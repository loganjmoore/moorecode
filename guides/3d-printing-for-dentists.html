3D printing for dentists and dental labs

Dental 3D printing is a controlled digital workflow that turns a scan or design into a physical model, pattern, guide, or appliance for a defined dental use. Printer, material, software, washing, drying, any required post-curing, inspection, and professional oversight must be treated as one system; a general-purpose hobby printer and resin are not substitutes for a validated patient-contact workflow.

Start with the intended dental application

A study model that never contacts a patient and a patient-contact appliance have different evidence requirements. Define the use first. The qualified dental professional or laboratory then selects the documented system, tolerances, processing, inspection, records, and applicable regulatory path for that use.

Dental 3D printing workflow

  1. Define the intended use and whether the result will contact a patient.
  2. Capture or receive the scan under the practice or lab's controlled process.
  3. Design the part and identify critical dimensions, fit, orientation, and traceability.
  4. Select the exact printer, material, software, and processing system approved for that use.
  5. Print without substituting a generic resin, profile, wash, or cure schedule.
  6. Wash, dry, post-cure when required, remove supports, and finish in the documented order.
  7. Inspect and record the result under the professional workflow before use.

Common applications and their boundaries

Models, demonstration pieces, patterns, guides, and appliances may all appear in dental printing, but the category name does not establish suitability. Accuracy, material behavior, patient contact, sterilization or disinfection requirements, traceability, and intended use decide the workflow. Use the source-checked resin dental guidance for the fuller safety and validation discussion.

Questions to answer before buying a printer

  • Which exact finished item will the practice or lab produce?
  • Which material and printer combination documents that intended use?
  • What wash, dry, cure, ventilation, PPE, waste, and inspection steps are required?
  • Who owns calibration, records, maintenance, and final professional acceptance?
  • Does the expected volume justify bringing the controlled workflow in-house?
