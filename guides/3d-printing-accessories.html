Best 3D printing accessories: a ranked starter kit

A 3D printing accessory is an add-on that solves a specific measuring, material, adhesion, cleanup, maintenance, or safety job. Start with digital calipers and cleanup tools, add dry storage next, and buy a different nozzle, build surface, dryer, or enclosure only when the printer and material require it.

3D printing accessories ranked by usefulness

  1. Digital calipers for checking filament, holes, walls, and finished fit.
  2. Flush cutters and a deburring tool for controlled support and edge cleanup.
  3. Build-surface cleaning supplies that match the printer maker's instructions.
  4. Dry filament storage with a closed container, desiccant, and humidity indicator.
  5. A material-specific nozzle when the filament is abrasive or the part needs a different line width.
  6. A compatible build surface when the material profile calls for a different adhesion or release method.
  7. A filament dryer or enclosure only for a diagnosed moisture or temperature-control need.

Which nozzle belongs in a starter kit?

Keep the printer's supported general-purpose nozzle until a real job requires another one. A smaller opening can trade speed for finer paths; a larger opening can trade detail for wider, faster paths. Abrasive filled materials may require a wear-resistant nozzle. Change the slicer profile to match the installed diameter and use only nozzle types the printer maker supports.

When are a filament dryer and dry box useful?

Dry storage prevents an opened spool from absorbing more moisture. A dryer is for material that already needs controlled drying under the filament maker's time and temperature instructions. Popping, rough extrusion, excess stringing, or a weak surface can have several causes, so confirm the material and profile before treating a dryer as a universal repair.

What should stay out of a generic tool bundle?

Skip mystery adhesives, sharp scrapers, unsupported nozzles, and tools with no defined job. The printer maker's build-surface method controls cleaning and release. Resin tools must remain in a separated dirty workflow; they do not belong in the FDM cleanup box.

Build the kit from the job backward

Choose the printer and material first, read their current instructions, then list the measurements, storage, support removal, surface preparation, and safety controls the job needs. The PLA, PETG, and TPU guide narrows the material choice, while the desktop printer guide covers workspace controls.
