3D printing accessories

The 3D printing accessories you actually need are digital calipers, flush cutters, a deburring tool, build-surface cleaning supplies approved by the printer maker, and closed dry filament storage. Add a different nozzle, build surface, filament dryer, or enclosure only when the printer, material, or a diagnosed problem requires it. Required 3D printing accessories

ItemWhy it mattersWhen to skip it
1. Digital calipersCheck filament, holes, walls, and finished fitSkip a second pair if you already own an accurate measuring tool
2. Flush cutters and deburring toolRemove supports and clean rough edges with controlSkip duplicates if the printer includes suitable cleanup tools
3. Approved build-surface cleanerKeep the plate clean using the printer maker's methodSkip a separate product when the maker specifies soap and water
4. Closed dry filament storageLimit moisture exposure after a spool is openedSkip a dedicated dry box if a closed container, desiccant, and humidity indicator are already available

ItemWhy it mattersWhen to skip it
5. Material-specific nozzleHandle abrasive filament or a job that needs another line widthKeep the supported general-purpose nozzle until a job requires a change
6. Compatible build surfaceUse an adhesion or release method supported for the materialKeep the supplied surface when its material profile works
7. Filament dryerDry material under the filament maker's instructionsSkip it when the material is dry and no moisture problem is diagnosed

Which nozzle belongs in a starter kit?

Keep the printer's supported general-purpose nozzle until a real job requires another one. A smaller opening can trade speed for finer paths; a larger opening can trade detail for wider, faster paths. Abrasive filled materials may require a wear-resistant nozzle. Change the slicer profile to match the installed diameter and use only nozzle types the printer maker supports.

When are a filament dryer and dry box useful?

Dry storage prevents an opened spool from absorbing more moisture. A dryer is for material that already needs controlled drying under the filament maker's time and temperature instructions. Popping, rough extrusion, excess stringing, or a weak surface can have several causes, so confirm the material and profile before treating a dryer as a universal repair.

What should stay out of a generic tool bundle?

Skip mystery adhesives, sharp scrapers, unsupported nozzles, and tools with no defined job. The printer maker's build-surface method controls cleaning and release. Resin tools must remain in a separated dirty workflow; they do not belong in the FDM cleanup box.

Build the kit from the job backward

Choose the printer and material first, read their current instructions, then list the measurements, storage, support removal, surface preparation, and safety controls the job needs.
