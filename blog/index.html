3D printing guides and project notes

The MooreCode blog is a collection of thirteen practical guides from a solo developer and maker. Ten cover 3D-printing processes, files, workspaces, materials, and projects; the other three cover software pricing, rules, and tools with numbers checked against named sources.

Choose a 3D printing guide by process or project

Use this guide selector to choose the process, material, workspace, or pricing article that answers your question. Each linked guide explains the tradeoffs and the next practical step rather than treating every printer or project as interchangeable.

Which 3D printing filament and materials should you start with?

Start with PLA filament for clean models and easy first prints. Choose PETG when a utility part needs more toughness or moderate heat resistance, and TPU when it must flex. The 3D printing materials guide compares all three and explains printing PETG.

Which 3D printing accessories belong in a basic tool kit?

A basic 3D printing tool kit needs calipers, flush cutters, a deburring tool, a clean build-surface tool, and dry spool storage. Buy a tool when a routine measurement, cleanup, or storage task requires it; skip large bundles padded with tools you will not use. See the ranked 3D printing accessories list.

What do home 3D printing and paint preparation require?

Home 3D printing needs a stable work surface, process-appropriate ventilation, safe power, clearance, material storage, and controlled post-processing. To paint an FDM print, remove supports, sand only where needed, clean the surface, apply a compatible primer, and test the paint on scrap first. Use the home 3D printing setup guide and the 3D printing paint and finishing steps.

How do FDM, SLA, SLS, PolyJet, and carbon 3D printing compare?

FDM melts filament, SLA cures liquid resin, and SLS fuses polymer powder. PolyJet jets and cures thin droplets of photopolymer, while Carbon's process uses digital light synthesis with proprietary resins. Start with the FDM, SLA, and SLS comparison for the common process tradeoffs; MooreCode does not yet have source-checked PolyJet or Carbon equipment guidance.

How do dentists and jewelry makers use resin 3D printing?

Resin 3D printing fits jewelry masters and dental models when small features and smooth surfaces justify the controlled wash, cure, ventilation, and waste workflow. Dental appliances and castable jewelry require a validated end-to-end material and processing system, not ordinary hobby resin. Read the dental 3D printing guidance or the 3D printing jewelry workflow.

How should a 3D printing business set its rates?

3D printing rates should cover material, machine time, labor, failure allowance, overhead, and profit instead of charging for filament alone. Use the 3D printing price calculator to build a per-part estimate, then review the calculator's 3D printing business pricing method.

Where does 3D printing fit in manufacturing and metal production?

3D printing in manufacturing fits low-volume parts, complex geometry, tooling, repair, and prototypes when its total delivered cost beats another process. Metal printing adds alloy qualification, supports, heat treatment, machining, and inspection to that decision. The 3D printing manufacturing guide compares four metal processes from the finished requirement backward.

3D printing filament types at a glance

Start with the job the part must do, then check that your printer, nozzle, build surface, and ventilation support the material. These six categories organize the material choices mentioned across MooreCode's guides.

Filament type Typical properties Typical uses
PLA Rigid, detailed, and approachable for first prints; limited around sustained heat Models, organizers, signs, and visual prototypes
PETG Tougher layer bonding and better moderate-heat performance than ordinary PLA; can string Brackets, holders, boxes, and utility parts
TPU Flexible and impact-absorbing; usually needs slower printing and a controlled feed path Feet, seals, grips, bumpers, and protective sleeves
ABS Tough and more heat-tolerant than PLA; needs careful temperature, enclosure, and ventilation planning Functional housings, fixtures, and workshop parts
Nylon Tough and wear-resistant; moisture control and printer capability matter Gears, hinges, clips, and durable moving parts
Specialty Filled, support, or decorative blends whose behavior depends on the base plastic and additives Stiff jigs, soluble supports, visual finishes, and material-specific experiments
