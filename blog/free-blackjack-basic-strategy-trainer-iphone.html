Free blackjack basic strategy trainer for iPhone compared

A basic strategy trainer is an app that quizzes you on the correct play for each blackjack hand against the dealer's up card. The free iPhone blackjack basic strategy trainers I checked split into two groups: apps that show ads or sell upgrades, and apps that ask you for nothing. Of the five I compared, only Blackjack Now is listed as free with no ads and no in-app purchases, and I build it, so read the table with that in mind. Use the rule settings column to decide, because a trainer only helps if its chart matches the table you will actually sit at.

Which free iPhone blackjack trainers have no ads?

Blackjack Masters - 21 Trainer, from F5 Games, says "No Ads" in its App Store listing. It is still free with in-app purchases, and the chip packages run from $0.99 to $69.99. Blackjack Trainer 101, from developer David Richmond, and Steve's Blackjack Trainer Pro, from Fuse Games, both carry a "Contains Advertising" label. Steve's also sells a VIP Premium upgrade for $2.99, and Blackjack Trainer 101 sells a Pro upgrade for the same $2.99.

Why do rule settings matter for basic strategy?

A basic strategy chart is built for a set of table rules, and changing one rule can change the right play for some hands. The most common example is whether the dealer hits or stands on soft 17. Blackjack Now and Blackjack Trainer 101 let you set that rule directly, and Blackjack Masters sets it per casino location, so you can drill the chart for the table you plan to play. A trainer with a fixed chart is fine for a first pass, but you will drill the wrong plays if your casino differs.

Is a free trainer worth it if it has ads?

It depends on how you study. An ad between hands is a small cost when you are drilling a few hundred hands, but it breaks the rhythm that makes repetition useful. Steve's Blackjack Trainer Pro carries both an advertising label and a paid upgrade, and its guided lessons suit you if you want a tutor voice more than a quiz. If you only need the chart and a quiz, a no-ad app with a visible chart is the simpler pick.

What should I check before I drill a trainer?

Run through four checks before you spend twenty minutes on any app:

  1. Set the rules to match your casino: number of decks, soft 17, double after split, and surrender.
  2. Find the chart. You want to glance at the correct play without leaving the app.
  3. Read the purchase label. "Free with in-app purchases" can mean a few dollars or a pile of chips.
  4. Confirm the device. Blackjack Trainer 101 says it is "Only for iPhone" and needs iOS 16.0 or later.

Blackjack Trainer: 21 Coach, by Aditya Appana, also came up in my search and is not in the table. Its listing says it is free with no ads and no in-app purchases, and it has a color-coded chart sorted by hard totals, soft totals, and pairs. It covers the same rule choices, so it is worth a look if you want a sixth option.

Frequently asked questions

Is Blackjack Now free with no ads?

Yes. The app's support page says the current version has no advertising, no in-app purchases, and no tracking permission prompt. It does not require an account.

Which free iPhone blackjack trainers include card counting?

Blackjack Masters - 21 Trainer has a card count toggle. Steve's Blackjack Trainer Pro includes Hi-Lo, Knock-Out, High Opt 1 and 2, and Omega 2 counting systems in its simulation mode.

Why is Blackjack Strategy Trainer! mostly blank in the table?

Its App Store page did not load when I checked, so I could not confirm its price, ads, purchases, or rule settings. Its search listing describes it as designed for iPad, which may not suit an iPhone.

Do I need to change the rules to match my casino?

Yes, if the trainer offers the option. Whether the dealer hits or stands on soft 17 changes some basic strategy plays, so a trainer that lets you match your table is more useful than one with a fixed chart.
