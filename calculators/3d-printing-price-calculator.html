Formula and units

Total direct cost = ((quantity × grams per part ÷ package grams × material price) + (quantity × print hours × average watts ÷ 1,000 × price per kWh) + (labor hours × labor rate) + (quantity × print hours × machine rate) + other direct costs) × (1 + failed-print allowance ÷ 100).

Price with markup = direct cost × (1 + markup ÷ 100).

Price for target gross margin = direct cost ÷ (1 - margin ÷ 100).

The watt-to-kilowatt-hour conversion follows the U.S. Department of Energy formula: kWh = watts × hours ÷ 1,000. Prusa Research's published calculator also treats material, electricity, labor, and machine or upkeep as separate editable inputs. See the Department of Energy energy-use lesson and Prusa's 3D-printing price calculator.

This is an estimate, not a quote. Add known costs in the available fields and confirm the final amount from the actual model, workflow, and supplier or customer requirements.

Worked fictional example

Suppose a two-part job uses 100 g per part from a 1,000 g spool priced at $25. Each part prints for four hours on a 120 W printer, electricity costs $0.15 per kWh, hands-on labor is 0.5 hours at $30 per hour, machine time is $1.50 per print hour, other direct costs are $3, and the failed-print allowance is 10%. Those editable assumptions produce a $38.66 direct cost. A 25% markup produces a $48.32 suggested price, $9.66 gross profit, and $24.16 per part.

3D printing price questions

How much should I charge for a 3D print?

Start with the direct cost of material, electricity, labor, machine time, failed-print allowance, and other known costs. Then use either a markup or target gross margin to calculate a suggested selling price. Confirm taxes, shipping, marketplace fees, and overhead separately before quoting a customer.

What is the difference between markup and margin?

Markup is profit expressed as a percentage of direct cost. Gross margin is gross profit expressed as a percentage of selling price. A 25% markup on a $100 direct cost produces a $125 price, while a 25% target gross margin produces a $133.33 price.

Is filament cost the same as the price of a 3D print?

No. Filament is one direct-cost input. A useful price estimate can also include electricity, hands-on labor, machine time, failed prints, hardware, packaging, and other costs that apply to the job.
